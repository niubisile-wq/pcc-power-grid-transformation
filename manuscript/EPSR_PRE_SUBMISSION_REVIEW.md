# EPSR pre-submission reviewer assessment

## Review setup

- **Input scope:** Full working manuscript, machine-generated Tables 1–4, frozen protocols, final evidence dashboard, claim–evidence matrix, and submission checklist.
- **Assessment boundary:** The numerical evidence and prose can be assessed. Final figures, figure legends, author metadata, CRediT/funding/conflict declarations, and an immutable public release are not yet available.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary:** A proof-carrying contract (PCC) binds task-relevant transformation evidence to a fail-closed execution gate so that semantically altered grid models cannot launch protected N-1, OPF, or SCOPF computations, while lawful transformations remain usable.
- **Visible evidence base:** 1,980 controlled transformations on 22 public networks; 56 AC N-1 attempts; 35 AC-OPF attempts; 50 DC-SCOPF states and 12,340 candidate-outage rows; official APL and bounded QoCDC controls; a ten-file untouched CGMES holdout; scaling to 13,659 assets; and an independent transformer-aligned solver comparison.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence:** Final figures and legends, a complete non-provisional bibliography, author-supplied declarations, and a public archive version that can be independently inspected.

## Reviewer 1

- **Overall assessment:** The manuscript presents a coherent and unusually auditable safety mechanism, and the completed DC-SCOPF campaign changes the work from a failure catalogue into a positive prevention study. The technical case is promising, but several implementation and inference boundaries need to be made more explicit before the evidence chain is fully reviewable.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** Researchers and engineers working on CGMES/CIM conversion, contingency analysis, OPF/SCOPF, model governance, and safety gates for optimization services would value a mechanism that prevents a valid numerical answer to the wrong transformed problem.
- **Major strengths:** The solver-launch endpoint is operationally meaningful; lawful and harmful transformations share a paired baseline ladder; denominators and failed solver pairs are retained; the five-network DC campaign is complete; all 369 strict false-secure releases are prevented; and the DCP/DCMP negative-control correction directly demonstrates why equation semantics matter.
- **Major concerns:** The threat model does not yet state clearly which components are trusted, especially the issuer, converter trace, task-contract author, key store, and verifier. The manuscript describes proof obligations but does not provide a compact soundness statement or algorithm that a reviewer can trace from certificate fields to the launch decision. The controlled mutations are generated within the same task vocabulary enforced by PCC, which creates a risk of benchmark–verifier alignment. The untouched holdout tests lawful and missing-proof behavior, but it does not supply a naturally occurring harmful conversion with an independently established operational consequence.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established:** Add an explicit threat/trust model; provide verifier and execution-gate pseudocode; state the conditional guarantee and its assumptions in formal terms; distinguish offline counterfactual consequence computation from the protected online launch; summarize the v2–v11 case500 amendment chain and show that scientific thresholds and candidate sets did not change; and explain why 38 invalid solver pairs and 50 exacerbated-overload rows are excluded from strict paired effects but retained in the attempted denominator.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** **Originality:** credible at the execution-boundary composition of task contracts, signed relation evidence, and fail-closed launch, although the distinction from provenance and model-transformation verification needs fuller citation support. **Scientific importance:** strong within interoperable power-system computation; broader importance depends on making the abstraction beyond CGMES explicit. **Interdisciplinary readership:** potentially relevant to safety-critical scientific computing and data pipelines, but currently framed mainly for power-system specialists. **Technical soundness:** strong empirical bookkeeping, with the trust model and proof argument still under-specified. **Readability for nonspecialists:** the problem is understandable, but the certificate/relation formalism is introduced too quickly and would benefit from one schematic.
- **Recommendation posture:** Supportive after a focused major revision that closes the trust-model, formal-method, and protocol-transparency gaps.

## Reviewer 2

- **Overall assessment:** The central idea is useful and the final experiments meet a credible EPSR-level bar, but novelty must be argued against adjacent work more sharply. The paper should avoid presenting every cryptographic or provenance component as individually new and instead isolate the new system-level proposition: task-semantic evidence is made an enforceable precondition for solver execution.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** The immediate audience is power-system model exchange and security-constrained optimization. A secondary audience includes researchers building learned optimization proxies or multi-organization model pipelines, because those systems also assume that preprocessing preserves the intended task.
- **Major strengths:** The baseline ladder shows a monotone residual risk that conventional controls do not close; the operational experiments report physical and economic consequences rather than mutation counts alone; the completed 5×10 DC grid provides breadth; and the independent formulation-alignment experiment turns an initial mismatch into mechanism evidence rather than hiding it.
- **Major concerns:** The related-work section is short and the bibliography remains provisional. The manuscript does not yet compare PCC systematically with provenance systems, proof-carrying data/code, certified compilation or semantics-preserving transformation, runtime policy enforcement, and grid-model validation beyond the few cited examples. The claim of “canonicalization” may be read as a conversion or normalization algorithm, whereas the implemented contribution appears primarily to be validation plus gated execution. The paper also needs a clearer account of what new knowledge is learned from the 369 strict false-secure cases beyond confirming that the synthetic omission families can be consequential.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established:** Expand and verify the literature map; define precisely whether PCC transforms, canonicalizes, certifies, or gates an existing transformation; add ablation interpretation rather than only counts; report effect heterogeneity across networks and operating states; and state why the minimal five-network sign-test result (p=0.03125) is appropriate evidence without implying population-wide prevalence.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** **Originality:** plausible but not yet cleanly separated from adjacent provenance and semantics-preservation techniques. **Scientific importance:** substantial practical importance for EPSR if positioned as prevention of false-secure execution, not as a universal formal-verification result. **Interdisciplinary readership:** the general “right solver, wrong problem” failure mode is broad, but that abstraction is underdeveloped. **Technical soundness:** the completed evidence gates are persuasive within scope; generalization remains bounded. **Readability for nonspecialists:** the abstract is clear and under 250 words, but terminology such as APL, QoCDC, BODF, and DCMP needs a more guided narrative.
- **Recommendation posture:** Promising and likely competitive for a specialist power-systems journal after novelty positioning and literature coverage are strengthened.

## Reviewer 3

- **Overall assessment:** The paper has a clear practical message and unusually transparent failure retention. Its current presentation, however, risks overwhelming the main story with protocol mechanics and standards acronyms. The final figures will be decisive for whether a reader can understand the method and the evidence hierarchy quickly.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** Operators, software vendors, standards groups, and researchers deploying optimization as a service would care because the method supplies an auditable reason to stop computation before a misleading result enters a workflow.
- **Major strengths:** The paper consistently separates structural conformance, task-semantic preservation, solver convergence, and operational consequence. It reports a successful solution—complete prevention in the tested scope—rather than centring the narrative on solver failures. The standards and untouched-source controls make the application setting concrete.
- **Major concerns:** The manuscript lacks final figures and legends, so the accessibility of the core argument cannot yet be assessed. The Results section contains several dense numerical paragraphs that need a visual hierarchy. The practical deployment description is still abstract: certificate issuance, validation latency, receipt handling, and operator response to `reject` versus `unresolved` should be shown as one end-to-end workflow. The Data and code availability section refers to an archive, but the final immutable version and reproduction instructions must be demonstrably available at submission.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established:** Produce a simple method schematic; visually distinguish lawful acceptance, harmful rejection, prevented operational consequence, and solver portability; add source-data mappings and statistical definitions to legends; provide a concise deployment example and failure-handling workflow; and complete the author and archive metadata.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** **Originality:** understandable from the manuscript, though the exact boundary from signed provenance needs reinforcement. **Scientific importance:** convincing for safe power-system model exchange and computation. **Interdisciplinary readership:** potentially strong if the “semantically wrong task” concept is visualized without CGMES-specific jargon. **Technical soundness:** supported by the machine-audited evidence, subject to the formal and trust-boundary issues raised above. **Readability for nonspecialists:** currently moderate; the abstract is accessible, but the body needs the planned schematic and more explicit acronym management.
- **Recommendation posture:** Supportive if the final visual narrative and deployment explanation make the contribution accessible without weakening the bounded claims.

## Cross-review synthesis

- **Consensus strengths:** All three reports view fail-closed solver launch as the paper’s strongest contribution. The complete DC-SCOPF denominator, strict paired effects, lawful controls, independent formulation comparison, untouched holdout, and transparent retention of failed amendments collectively support a positive EPSR-style application paper.
- **Consensus technical risks:** The trust/threat model and conditional guarantee are not explicit enough; novelty is not yet distinguished from adjacent provenance and transformation-verification work; case500 solver amendments need a concise transparent account; and final figures, legends, archive metadata, and bibliography are incomplete.
- **Where emphasis differs across reviewers:** Reviewer 1 weights formal assumptions, benchmark–verifier alignment, and protocol validity most heavily. Reviewer 2 weights novelty, prior-work separation, and inference scope. Reviewer 3 weights deployment clarity, visual hierarchy, and nonspecialist readability.
- **Broad-interest / significance readout:** The evidence supports strong field-level significance for EPSR. Broader relevance is plausible through the general failure mode of obtaining a valid answer to a semantically changed task, but the manuscript should present that implication as a transferable design pattern rather than claim universal validation.
- **Most important issues to resolve before a strong case is established:** (1) add the trust model and compact formal guarantee; (2) expand and verify the prior-work comparison; (3) document the frozen scientific design versus numerical solver amendments; (4) produce five source-traceable figures with complete legends; and (5) finish the immutable public release and author-supplied declarations.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- A universal guarantee for all CGMES transformations, profiles, solvers, or operational workflows is not supported; the manuscript currently states this boundary correctly and must retain it.
- Real-world attack prevalence or blackout-risk reduction is not assessable from controlled mutations and public benchmarks.
- Utility-production deployment, key revocation, compromised issuers, and multi-party governance are not demonstrated.
- Full QoCDC compliance and global APL conformance of the untouched holdout are not supported.
- Final figure readability, image integrity, and source-data traceability are not assessable until the figure package exists.
- The final archive DOI/version, author contributions, funding, and conflict declarations require author confirmation.
